MailHub

A private e-mail management program, used by Toma Rendic on one computer. It is not offered to the public.

MailHub shows the mail of our own mailboxes in one place, keeps it organised, reminds us of mail that needs an answer, and lets us reply. It runs on one computer and keeps everything it knows on that computer.

Privacy policy

Last updated 11 October 2026

What MailHub asks Google for

When one of our Gmail mailboxes is connected with Google sign-in, MailHub asks for permission to read, send, delete and manage the mail in that mailbox (the Gmail permission https://mail.google.com/) and for the e-mail address of the account. It uses this to show, organise, delete, archive and reply to the mail of that mailbox through Gmail's standard IMAP and SMTP services. It only ever acts on mailboxes we own and connected ourselves.

What it keeps, and where

  • The sign-in token Google issues is kept in Windows Credential Manager on the computer that runs MailHub. It is never written to a file, a log or a database.
  • Mail and the notes we make about it are kept in MailHub's own database on that same computer.
  • Nothing is uploaded to any other server, sold, or shared with anyone.

Third parties

If the owner has set up MailHub's AI feature, pressing "Summarize" or "Draft a reply" sends the text of that one conversation to the AI provider the owner configured (Anthropic) for that request only. Nothing is sent otherwise.

MailHub's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Taking permission back

Disconnecting a mailbox in MailHub revokes its permission at Google and deletes the token. The permission can also be removed at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
